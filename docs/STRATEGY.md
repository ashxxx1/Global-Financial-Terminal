# Positioning & Strategy

Growth-plan items 34–37. This is the document that settles *who this is for* —
every roadmap and copy decision should be checkable against it.

## 34. The ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)

> **Globally-curious retail investors and finance-adjacent professionals —
> people who follow markets seriously as an interest but not as a job, and who
> care about the world beyond the S&P 500.**

Concretely, three overlapping groups:

| Segment | Who they are | Why they're a fit |
|---|---|---|
| **The macro-curious investor** | Has a brokerage account and real money in it. Reads FT/Economist/Bloomberg headlines. Invests on 1–5 year horizons, not intraday. | Wants context and orientation, not execution tooling. Willing to pay for clarity. |
| **The non-US market watcher** | Lives in India, Brazil, SEA, Middle East, Europe. Follows their local market *and* global flows. | **Underserved.** Almost every free tool is US-first. Our 68-country coverage is genuinely differentiated for them. |
| **Finance-adjacent professionals & students** | Analysts, consultants, finance students, fintech/startup people. Need to sound informed about markets. | High engagement, high share propensity, become advocates. Cheap to reach via community. |

### Explicitly NOT our customer

Saying no is what makes the ICP useful:

- **Day traders / active traders.** Need real-time tick data, Level 2, execution
  and broker integration. We cannot legally or economically serve this, and
  chasing them is the trap described in the plan's anti-goals.
- **Institutional / professional desks.** They have Bloomberg and Refinitiv.
  We will never out-feature them.
- **Pure passive index investors.** Buy VOO, check once a year. No recurring
  need, no reason to pay.
- **People who want stock picks.** We provide context, not recommendations —
  and legally must not.

## 35. Jobs to be done

The three jobs, in priority order. Every feature should serve one of them.

### JTBD 1 — Orientation (the daily job)
> *"When I check in on markets, help me understand what happened in the world
> and whether any of it matters to me — in under two minutes."*

This is the **habit-forming** job and the one that drives retention. Served by:
the globe, market sessions, breadth, sector heat map, watchlist, and
(critically, once built) the daily digest.

### JTBD 2 — Interpretation (the event job)
> *"When something big happens, help me understand which markets and sectors it
> actually hits, so I know if it's noise or signal."*

This is the **differentiating** job — the news→sector→stock engine is the only
thing here that TradingView and Yahoo genuinely don't do. Served by: the news
engine, country pages, connections graph.

### JTBD 3 — Competence (the social job)
> *"Help me feel — and sound — informed about global markets without paying for
> a terminal."*

This is the **sharing** job and therefore the growth engine. It's why the
product being beautiful is a business asset, not vanity. Served by: the globe,
shareable snapshots, the visual quality of everything.

## 36. Positioning statement

> **For** globally-curious investors and finance-adjacent professionals
> **who** want to understand what's moving world markets without paying for a
> professional terminal or squinting at US-centric tables,
> **GFT Terminal is** a living visual map of the global economy
> **that** shows what's moving, where, and *why* — on one screen.
>
> **Unlike** TradingView (built for people who already know what to look at) or
> Yahoo Finance (comprehensive, US-first, and unpleasant to use),
> **we** lead with global context and explain the "so what" by mapping news to
> the sectors and countries it hits.

**The one-line version:** *The most beautiful way to understand what the world's
economy is doing right now.*

**Category we're competing in:** not "trading platform" — **market awareness /
financial media-tool**. Our real competitor for attention is doomscrolling
financial Twitter, not a charting package.

## 37. Competitors and our honest wedge

| Competitor | What they're genuinely better at | Our wedge | Our honest weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TradingView** | Charting, indicators, community scripts, real-time data, execution links. The default for anyone who trades. | They serve people who already know *what* to look at. We serve orientation — what's happening globally and why. Their world/macro view is an afterthought. | They are vastly more capable on data and charts. We will not win a feature war. |
| **Yahoo Finance** | Enormous free coverage, brand trust, portfolio tracking, real quotes. | Design, global-first framing, no ad clutter, and the news→sector linkage. Yahoo tells you a headline exists; we tell you what it hits. | Free, entrenched, huge SEO moat. We must out-*niche* them, not out-scale them. |
| **Finviz** | The best-known market heat map, powerful screener, fast. | Global coverage vs their US-only focus; far better design; news linkage. | Their screener and US depth are better. Their SEO is strong. |
| **Investing.com** | Comprehensive global coverage, economic calendar, many languages. | Design and signal-to-noise. Their UI is dense and ad-heavy; ours is legible. | They already own the global-coverage SEO we're targeting. Realistic assessment: they are our most direct SEO competitor. |
| **Koyfin** | Institutional-grade data and screens for serious retail. | Price point and approachability; visual orientation vs data density. | Much deeper data. They own the "prosumer" tier we'd graduate users into. |

### The strategic read

Our moat is **not** data — we will always lose that fight. Our moat is
**visual orientation + global-first framing + design**, which compounds into a
brand rather than a feature. The globe is the wedge; the news→sector engine is
the reason to stay; the country pages are the SEO surface.

**The honest risk:** design is copyable. Speed and community are the real
defence, not secrecy.
